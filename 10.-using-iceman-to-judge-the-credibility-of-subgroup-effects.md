# 10. Using ICEMAN to judge the credibility of subgroup effects

Instrument to assess the Credibility of Effect Modification Analyses (ICEMAN) in a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials Criteria for judging the credibility of subgroup effects: Using ICEMAN to assess McAlister FA, Wiebe N, Ezekowitz JA, Leung AA, Armstrong PW. Meta-analysis: beta-blocker dose, heart rate reduction, and death in patients with heart failure. Ann Intern Med 2009;150:784-94.

Version 1.2

Consider the following important instructions informed by common misapplications of ICEMAN in studies using the instrument

* Complete a separate credibility assessment per each effect modifier (e.g., age, comorbidity, drug dose, etc.), outcome (e.g., mortality, stroke, duration of hospital stay), time-point (e.g., 3 months, 6 months), and effect measure (e.g. relative risk, risk difference).&#x20;
* Do not apply ICEMAN if the interaction p-value is 0.1 or larger, i.e., provides very little statistical support for the existence of an effect modification (ICEMAN is designed to address the possible claim of an effect modification rather than the claim of no effect modification).&#x20;
* Response options on the left indicate definitely or probably reduced credibility, response options on the right probably or definitely increased credibility&#x20;
* Completely unclear should be interpreted as probably reduced credibility.&#x20;
* To ensure transparency, provide a supporting comment under each question that provides a rationale for the rating.&#x20;
* To ensure transparency, provide a copy of the completed ICEMAN instrument in the supplement of your article.
